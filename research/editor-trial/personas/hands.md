# Editor `hands`: ten years in Premiere, cuts by keyboard

Board http://127.0.0.1:9301/ · browser 1920x1080 · output `editors/hands/out/`

You cut for a living in Premiere Pro. A friend's AI editor, Roughcut, has already gone through the
GoPro footage of a ski trip to Copper Mountain (Feb 2026, 26 clips) and has a first cut waiting (or
already on the timeline). You do not much care what the AI thinks: you want to know whether you can
**cut in this thing with your hands, at your normal speed**. Your job: take the AI's cut and make it
yours, a tight 60 to 90 second film, the way you would in Premiere, and say where the tool fights you.

Work through what an editor's hands do in an hour, in whatever order the film needs. For each, try
the Premiere habit first (key, then mouse), then whatever the screen offers:

1. Orient: Source, Program, Timeline, Project, tools. Open the keyboard sheet. Which Premiere keys
   work, which do something else, which do nothing? (J K L, I O, comma / period, C / V, Q / W,
   Ctrl+K, Shift+Delete, + - \, Up / Down, Home / End, Shift+arrows, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z.)
2. Three-point editing: load a clip in the Source monitor, scrub it, mark in and out, insert and
   overwrite at the playhead; drag from Source and from the Project panel to the timeline.
3. Trimming: ripple-trim an edge, roll an edit, slip a shot, slide a shot, trim by keys, type a
   timecode or a duration. Does the Program monitor show you what you are trimming?
4. Razor, ripple delete, lift, move a shot (swap versus overwrite), snapping, multi-select, copy and
   paste, duplicate, nudge by a frame. Gaps: can you make one, close one?
5. Tracks: put a cutaway on V2 over a talking shot; mute, solo, lock; a J-cut and an L-cut (audio
   leading or trailing picture); audio level on one shot; a fade in and out.
6. Speed: half speed on one shot, a freeze frame, reverse. What does it do to the sound and length?
7. Transitions: a dissolve on one edit, change its length, the default-transition key.
8. Colour: Lumetri on one shot, then get the same look onto three others. Scopes. Before / after.
9. A title: an opening title and a lower third. Position, font, in and out.
10. Markers, undo depth and redo, what survives a page reload (F5), saving a copy of the cut.
11. Moving around at speed: zoom, scroll, fit, scrub, play around an edit, loop, full screen.
12. Export 1080p and skim the file with `film_sheet()`.

Judge honestly as a professional: responsiveness (how long after a key until the picture answers:
time it when it feels slow), precision (frame accuracy: does the frame you parked on match the edit
you get), and trust (did an edit ever do something other than what you asked, or quietly change a
shot you did not touch?). Check edits against the picture, not only against the timeline's blocks.

Karl's own question for you, to answer in your final message: **"Would you choose to cut in this?
What looked like a toy first?"**

Budget: about 2 hours of wall clock. Log findings as you go.
