# Rough-Cut Quality Rubric (draft v1 — pending calibration)

Eight criteria, scored 1–5. Anchors are written for **rough cuts**, not finished videos: color,
music, titles, and transitions are out of scope in Phase 1 and must not be scored here.

Status: **draft.** Calibration against real reference videos (R6 method step 2) has not run, so
the pass bar at the bottom is provisional.

---

### 1. Coverage — is anything important missing?
- **1** — A moment you'd consider essential is absent entirely.
- **3** — All the obvious beats are present; one or two secondary moments you'd have included are missing.
- **5** — Everything you'd have picked yourself is in there, plus at least one thing you'd forgotten about.

### 2. Selection — when several takes cover the same thing, is the best one used?
- **1** — Visibly worse takes chosen over better ones (shakier, worse framing, obstructed).
- **3** — Mostly reasonable choices; a couple of places where a better alternative existed in the bin.
- **5** — Consistently picks the strongest available take, including when the difference is subtle.

### 3. Pacing — do shots run the right length?
- **1** — Shots overstay badly or flash past; you reach for the scrub bar.
- **3** — Broadly watchable; some shots run a beat or two long, or a good moment gets clipped short.
- **5** — Shot lengths feel deliberate — action shots let you see the action, quiet shots breathe without dragging.

### 4. Structure — does the ordering make sense?
- **1** — Feels shuffled; chronology or logic is violated in ways that confuse.
- **3** — Sensible order, roughly chronological, but no shape — it's a sequence, not an arc.
- **5** — There's a discernible beginning, middle and end, and the ordering does work the raw chronology wouldn't.

### 5. Cut cleanliness — are the cut points themselves well-placed?
- **1** — Cuts land mid-word, mid-action, or on camera movement in a way that reads as broken.
- **3** — Cuts are technically fine; a few land awkwardly but nothing jarring.
- **5** — Cuts land on natural boundaries — end of a phrase, completion of an action, a settle in the motion.

### 6. Audio comfort — can you listen to it without wincing?
- **1** — Level jumps between clips, audible clicks at cuts, or dialogue that can't be made out.
- **3** — Consistent enough to watch; some wind or level variation you'd fix before showing anyone.
- **5** — Uniform loudness, clean cut boundaries, speech intelligible throughout.

### 7. Goal fit — does it serve the stated brief?
- **1** — Ignores the brief; a different goal would have produced the same cut.
- **3** — Consistent with the brief but doesn't lean into it; generic for the footage.
- **5** — You can tell what it was optimizing for. A different brief would clearly have produced a different cut.

### 8. Length discipline — is it near the target duration?
- **1** — More than 50% over or under target.
- **3** — Within ~25% of target.
- **5** — Within ~10% of target, and the length feels earned rather than padded or truncated.

---

## Scoring sheet
Score each criterion 1–5, note the specific timestamp that drove the score, and write one line
on what would raise it. Timestamps matter more than the numbers — they're what the next
iteration acts on.

## Provisional pass bar (to be replaced by calibration)
Mean ≥ 3.5 across the eight criteria, with **no single criterion below 2**. The no-criterion-below-2
rule exists because a rough cut with great selection and unlistenable audio is not a usable
rough cut, and an average would hide that.

**Calibration will set the real bar.** Method: score two or three of your own past edits and one
deliberately bad auto-cut (random shots concatenated to target length). If the bad cut doesn't
score clearly worse across most criteria, the anchors are broken and get rewritten before the
bar is set. The pass bar should land relative to where your own edits score, not at an absolute
number chosen in advance.
