Roughcut · effects · design note · 2026-09-20 · INTAKE M12

Effects the model designs and the machine verifies.

Karl's ask: tell the AI "add Call of Duty hit markers where my skis hit the rocks, with the sound effect", have it generate the overlay and the sound itself, iterate with it, and have it prove the definition of done — the sound lands with the marker, on the right frames — with a way to draw a reference on a frame. This note lists the ways that were considered, says which one was built and why, and lays out the alternatives, with the free online and AI tools that fit each.

01

Seven ways to make an effect appear

The question splits in three: how the picture of the effect is made, how its sound is made, and how both are anchored to the right frame and the right place. Every approach below answers at least one of the three; most answer one well and the others badly.

A · A program the model writes BUILT

The model does not produce pixels or samples. It writes a small effect program in a closed vocabulary — a sprite of shapes in a unit box with keyframed scale, opacity, rotation and offset, plus a synth patch of tone, noise, click and sweep layers with envelopes — and Roughcut's own code draws both, on the master (PIL + numpy) and on the monitor (canvas + WebAudio), from one JSON.

  • Free, deterministic, resolution-independent, no licence, no GPU, seconds to render.
  • Iterable by numbers: "red and bigger" changes two fields, not a file.
  • Verifiable: the machine can read what it drew.
  • Limits: no photoreal smoke or sampled real-world sound; the look is graphic, like a game HUD — which is what a hit marker is.

B · Generative image and video models LATER

A diffusion model makes the overlay: a transparent sprite (SDXL or FLUX in ComfyUI with a LayerDiffuse or matting node) or a short clip (Stable Video Diffusion, AnimateDiff, Wan 2.x). Hosted: Runway, Pika, Luma, Kling.

  • Rich visuals — sparks, dust, a lens crack.
  • Alpha is the hard part: most models emit an opaque frame; matting after the fact is where it breaks.
  • Non-deterministic, GPU-hungry, and the checklist cannot read intent from pixels.

C · Generative audio NEXT, OPTIONAL

A text-to-sound model makes the sample: Stable Audio Open (local, free weights), Meta AudioCraft / AudioGen (local, free), ElevenLabs Sound Effects (hosted, free tier).

  • Real-sounding impacts where the synth patch is too clean.
  • Fits the built loop as one more layer type — sample — verified the same way (the transient still has to land).
  • Local weights keep it free; hosted has quotas.

D · A library of assets

EFFECTS.md's original Rule 4: the model picks from files in assets/ described by a manifest. Sources: Freesound (CC), Pixabay, Mixkit, OpenGameArt.

  • Real recordings, instantly.
  • Licensing bookkeeping is the human's; search quality is the ceiling; nothing is ever "generated by the AI".
  • Still supported: a sample layer can point at a library file as well as a generated one.

E · Code the model writes, rendered headless

The model writes a Remotion / Motion Canvas / p5.js sketch or a Manim scene; a headless browser renders frames.

  • Unlimited expressiveness.
  • Executable code from a model, arriving by way of a transcript — the injection path EFFECTS.md Rule 1 exists to close. Needs a sandbox and a heavier stack.

F · Vision tracking for anchoring NEXT

Track the skis through the shot so the marker follows them: CoTracker or SAM 2 (local, free), or OpenCV optical flow.

  • The anchor moves with the thing, not just sits where a frame was placed.
  • Fits the vocabulary as-is: the tracker writes dx/dy keyframes.

G · Do it in an NLE

DaVinci Resolve (free) Fusion templates, CapCut (free) effects, After Effects with Firefly.

  • Off the board, hand-placed, no verification, no conversation. Kept here as the baseline the built path has to beat.
02

What was built: A, with the human's drawing as the strongest signal

The four rules of docs/EFFECTS.md hold, with one turned inside out: the model never writes ffmpeg (Rule 1), nothing is in pixels (Rule 2), precision comes from narrowing (Rule 3), and the asset is generated, not fetched (Rule 4, inverted). The vocabulary, the validation and the loop live in roughcut/fx.py and /api/fx; the board draws the same JSON on the monitor.

1 · SAYThe note

"hit markers where my skis hit the rocks, with the sound" — in the FX tool, on the selected shot. Or draw on the frame first.

2 · NARROWFind the impacts

The onset track (10 Hz, already in every audio sidecar) gives the candidate strikes inside the shot. No model call.

3 · DESIGNThe program

One text call: the sprite and the synth patch, in the vocabulary, validated hard. A drawn reference's marks become the anchors directly.

4 · PLACEWhere the skis are

Optional, priced on the button: a strip of frames at each candidate, one call, x/y per hit. Or two clicks on the monitor.

5 · PREVIEWOn the monitor

The same JSON drawn live over the proxy with its sound. Nudge a hit by a frame; move an anchor by a click.

6 · VERIFYThe checklist

A proof render of the one shot, with and without; the machine measures. Iterate with the model until it passes.

7 · ACCEPTInto the EDL

Only the human's click writes effects. The render bakes the overlay and the mix into that shot's part.

The vocabulary, in one example

{"name": "hit markers", "shot": "gd6a5fe3527", "clip": "CLIP_08.MP4",
 "events": [{"t": 268.8, "x": 0.52, "y": 0.71}, {"t": 271.1, "x": 0.49, "y": 0.73}],
 "overlay": {"duration": 0.35, "size": 0.12,
   "shapes": [{"type": "line", "from": [-1,-1], "to": [-0.3,-0.3], "width": 0.1}, … four ticks in an X …],
   "anim": {"scale": [[0,1.4],[0.06,1.0]], "opacity": [[0,1],[0.23,1],[0.35,0]]},
   "flash": {"color": "#ff0000", "opacity": 0.15, "duration": 0.08}},
 "sound": {"duration": 0.18, "gain_db": -6,
   "layers": [{"type": "tone", "freq": 1800, "wave": "square", "decay": 0.06},
              {"type": "noise", "color": "white", "hp": 1500, "decay": 0.09}]}}

Times are clip seconds, anchors are fractions of the frame, the sprite's box is a fraction of the frame width. The same JSON draws on the 720p proxy in the monitor and on the 4K master. A "make them red and bigger" is a revise call that returns the same shape with two numbers changed.

The definition of done, as the machine runs it

✓ in_shot · every hit inside the shot's range
✓ in_frame · every anchor inside the frame, the sprite not more than half off an edge
✓ sync · the sound and the overlay share every t; the sound is not longer than the overlay + 0.25 s
✓ on_onset · each hit within 150 ms of an onset peak (when the note names an impact)
✓ audio_landed · the proof's audio rises ≥ 6 dB at each hit against the 300 ms before it
✓ picture_landed · the proof's frame differs from the base at each hit, and the change's box contains the anchor
– on_the_skis · priced: the model looks at the proof's frames and says whether the marker sits on the skis

The first four are free and instant. The two measured ones need the proof render — seconds on the proxy, the one shot only — and they are what makes "verified" a fact rather than a sentence: the machine decodes the part's audio and frames and reads what it put there. The sixth costs a call and is the human's to spend.

Drawing a reference

Pause the monitor on the frame, press Draw a reference, mark the skis (one stroke per hit), write the goal ("markers here, when the skis hit"), press Use it. The frame with the marks is saved as a PNG next to the effect; the marks' centroids become the anchors; the frame's time snaps to the nearest onset peak; the model is asked only for the look. Two strokes beat any amount of inference, and the reference stays with the effect as its provenance.

The order that pays

  • Tracking (F) next: the one thing a hit marker visibly lacks when the skis move during the 350 ms is following them. CoTracker on the proof frames, writing dx/dy keyframes — the vocabulary already has the fields.
  • A sampled sound layer (C) when the synth is too clean for a given effect: Stable Audio Open locally, a sample layer, the same check. The library path (D) comes for free with it.
  • Image sprites (B, stills only) for effects that want a texture the shapes cannot draw: a PNG with alpha from ComfyUI, an image shape, animation still keyframed and verifiable.
  • Generated clips (B, video) and model-written code (E) last, and only with a matte step and a sandbox respectively. Both cost more than they add for a HUD-style effect, which is most of what a ski film wants.
